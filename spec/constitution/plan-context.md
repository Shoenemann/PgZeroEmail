# ZeroEmail Context Plan

## Setting

The story moves between the fictional Italian coastal town of Bellafonte and the nearby Alpine city of Monteluce. The locations are invented so the game can borrow the warmth, food, architecture, and rhythms of Italy without making claims about a real institution or historical event.

## Protagonist

**Marta Bellini** is a celebrated investigative journalist known for following small clues that larger institutions ignore. She is sharp, fast, and accustomed to working alone. Her weakness is that she treats every problem as a professional investigation, including problems that need patience and a conversation.

Marta is travelling abroad to investigate the disappearance of the **Luce di San Vito**, a fictional ceremonial glass lantern believed to symbolize safe returns for sailors. She relies on her inbox to coordinate sources, family, transport, and evidence. The player controls Marta's email decisions, not her physical movement.

Marta is the aunt of **Nico**, an inventive Italian child who loves puzzles, secret names, and dramatic treasure hunts. Nico is not malicious. His involvement is revealed as an overenthusiastic attempt to protect a family surprise and create a mystery worthy of Marta.

## Supporting characters

- **Nico Bellini:** Marta's young nephew and the eventual source of the missing lantern's disappearance. His messages are earnest, funny, and full of suspiciously theatrical clues.
- **Giulia Bellini:** Marta's sister and Nico's mother. She wants to tell Marta the truth but keeps protecting Nico from the consequences of his stunt.
- **Luca Ferri:** A local museum conservator and Marta's old friend. He provides provenance details and becomes frustrated when Marta reads every delay as evidence of a conspiracy.
- **Sofia Conti:** A practical travel producer who keeps Marta on schedule, labels everything, and sends the game’s most useful operational reminders.
- **Professor Ada Rinaldi:** A retired historian whose fragmented notes point Marta toward the lantern’s history and a false trail.
- **Enzo:** The Bellafonte café owner, an enthusiastic source of gossip and an unreliable sender of messages containing far too many exclamation marks.

## Storyline witnessed by the player

### Opening: The assignment

Marta receives a request to investigate the missing Luce di San Vito while travelling outside Italy. Her inbox is crowded with routine work, family notes, and a few urgent messages. The player learns the basic inbox loop and begins separating signal from noise.

### Rising mystery

Messages from Luca and Professor Rinaldi suggest that the lantern was moved shortly before an exhibition. A travel delay, an incomplete archive, and a message sent from an unexpected address make the disappearance look like an international theft. Marta's professional instincts intensify the search.

### Personal complication

Giulia asks Marta to stop interrogating the family group chat. Nico sends cryptic clues that are funny but oddly precise. The player must manage replies, forwarded evidence, labels, stars, and saved threads while Marta's assumptions begin to affect her relationships.

### False certainty

A batch of messages appears to identify a culprit. The evidence is actually a chain of misunderstandings: a museum inventory label, Nico's alias, and a forwarded message stripped of context. Marta must review whole conversations rather than act on the newest line.

### Revelation

The advanced navigation levels reveal that Nico borrowed the lantern's replica, hid it as the centrepiece of a treasure hunt, and accidentally caused the real exhibition mix-up when he swapped two labelled cases. Giulia helped conceal the mistake temporarily. No adult conspiracy exists.

### Resolution

Marta returns to Bellafonte, explains the consequences to Nico, and publishes a piece about care, memory, and the difference between a mystery and a mess. The lantern is recovered, the family admits where communication failed, and Marta learns to investigate with people rather than only through them.

## Message taxonomy

- **Story-critical messages:** authored by the named cast, tagged in content data, and necessary for understanding the mystery or character arc.
- **Quest messages:** fictional work mail used to create objectives and repeat shortcut patterns.
- **Humour messages:** optional inbox texture, such as Enzo's café logistics or an aggressively detailed email about pastries.
- **Practice messages:** low-stakes synthetic conversations used by tutorials, drills, and replay mode.

Story-critical messages must never be hidden behind a random chance or made impossible to recover after an error. Humour should support scanning and mood without mocking disability, nationality, or the player's mistakes.

## Character arc

Marta begins as an efficient but emotionally distant investigator. Across seven levels she learns that speed is valuable only when paired with context, reversibility, and attention to people. Nico learns that a clever secret can still create real work for others. Giulia learns to communicate directly instead of managing everyone's feelings through omissions. The ending is warm, accountable, and playful rather than punitive.
